I've spent a lot of time working on other people's projects.

Writing content. Building websites. Improving SEO. Managing digital marketing. Testing AI tools. Setting up automations. Helping businesses figure out how to get more customers online.

But lately, I've become more interested in something else:

**Building my own things.**

Not just having ideas. Actually turning those ideas into working products, systems, experiments, and businesses.

That's what this series is about.

## Why I'm documenting the process

I have a bad habit that I suspect a lot of people building things can relate to.

I get an idea.

Then another one.

Then another.

Before long, I have a list of products I could build, tools I want to learn, businesses I want to test, and workflows I want to automate.

The problem isn't generating ideas.

The problem is shipping them.

So I'm changing the approach.

Instead of waiting until something is finished before talking about it, I'm going to document the process while I'm building.

That means writing about the decisions, experiments, technical problems, marketing attempts, things that work, and things that completely fail.

Some of these projects will become products.

Some will become businesses.

Some will probably be abandoned.

That's part of the point.

## What I'm building

My interests sit at the intersection of a few different areas:

* AI and AI-assisted workflows
* Automation and tools like n8n
* SEO and content systems
* Digital marketing
* Small-business growth
* SaaS and lightweight web applications
* Personal branding
* Systems that reduce repetitive work

I'm particularly interested in building relatively simple products that solve specific problems.

Not another app with 47 features.

Not an AI wrapper that exists because someone needed to put "AI" in the landing page.

Useful software.

Useful systems.

Things that save someone time, make money, remove repetitive work, or make a difficult process easier.

One example I'm currently exploring is an **AI Job Search OS for remote professionals**.

The basic idea is simple: instead of manually searching multiple job boards, filtering opportunities, researching companies, deciding whether a role is actually relevant, and then starting every application from scratch, why not build a system that handles as much of that workflow as possible?

I'm also experimenting with marketing systems, automation workflows, small-business growth services, and other product ideas.

Some of these projects will stay private until they're ready.

Others will be documented here from the beginning.

## The constraint is intentional

I'm not approaching this with a huge budget or a team of engineers.

That's actually useful.

Constraints force decisions.

If I only have a limited amount of time each day, I can't spend six months building a feature nobody wants.

If I have almost no budget, I need to find ways to validate an idea before spending money.

If I can't build something myself, I need to figure out whether AI-assisted development, automation, or existing tools can get me most of the way there.

And if the answer is still no, maybe the idea isn't worth building.

That's a much better question than:

> "How can I build this?"

The question I'm trying to ask first is:

> **"Should this exist, and can I prove that before I spend months building it?"**

## What you'll find here

This won't be a traditional tech blog.

I'm not interested in pretending every experiment is a success.

I'll write about:

**Building**

What I'm making, how I'm approaching the architecture, what tools I'm using, and why I made certain decisions.

**Automation**

The workflows I'm creating with tools like n8n, especially where automation can replace repetitive manual work.

**AI**

How I'm actually using AI in products and business workflows, including where it works well and where it doesn't.

**Marketing**

Experiments around SEO, content, paid advertising, lead generation, personal branding, and customer acquisition.

**Business**

What happens when an idea moves from "this could be cool" to "will someone actually pay for this?"

**Failures**

Bad ideas, abandoned projects, technical mistakes, poor assumptions, and experiments that don't work.

Those might end up being some of the most useful posts.

## The goal

I don't have a master plan where I already know which project will work.

I'm going to build, test, learn, and adjust.

The goal is to become better at taking an idea from:

**Problem → Idea → Prototype → Validation → Product → Business**

And to document enough of that process that someone else can learn from it too.

If something works, I'll explain what I did.

If it fails, I'll explain why I think it failed.

And if I change direction, I'll explain what caused the change.

This is the beginning of the series.

Now I just need to build something worth writing about.
